JARVIS Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026

JARVIS is a Windows desktop application for working with Claude Code. This page describes what JARVIS does with your data, specifically regarding its optional Google Drive integration and in general.

Who this covers

This policy covers JARVIS, a desktop application you install and run on your own PC. It is published by the maintainer of github.com/Silentt9879/JarvisApp. JARVIS has no server component, no account system of its own, and no backend that your data passes through.

Google Drive integration

JARVIS's Notes feature can optionally connect to your Google Drive to back up, restore, and synchronize the notes you write in JARVIS. This connection is entirely optional and off by default — nothing is connected unless you explicitly press "Connect" yourself.

What JARVIS can access

JARVIS requests only the drive.file scope from Google. This scope is deliberately narrow: it grants access only to files and folders that JARVIS itself creates in your Drive. JARVIS can never see, list, read, or modify any file already in your Drive, or any file created by another app or person — Google enforces this restriction on its own servers, independent of anything JARVIS's code does.

What JARVIS does with that access

  • Creates a folder in your Drive (named "JARVIS Knowledge Backups" or "JARVIS Notes Sync") containing copies of the notes you write in JARVIS, so you can back them up, restore them, or synchronize them between your own devices.
  • Never uploads, reads, or otherwise touches any other file or folder in your Drive.
  • Never sends your notes, your Drive contents, or any other data from your PC to JARVIS's developer, or to any third party. The only parties your data ever travels to are Google's own servers (when you choose to back up or sync) and, separately and only if you explicitly use JARVIS's AI features, Anthropic's Claude API (for the specific content you choose to send in a chat message — never your whole notes database, and never automatically).

Where your data is stored

  • Locally: your notes, settings, and Google account connection token are stored on your own PC, under your Windows user profile (%APPDATA%\JARVIS). Nothing is stored on any server operated by JARVIS's developer, because no such server exists.
  • Your Google account token is encrypted at rest using Windows' own per-user encryption (via Electron's safeStorage API), the same mechanism Windows uses to protect other applications' saved credentials. It is never stored in plain text, and never leaves your PC except to Google's own token-refresh servers, directly from your PC.
  • In Google Drive: only the notes you explicitly back up or sync, inside the JARVIS-created folder described above.

Revoking access

You can disconnect JARVIS from your Google account at any time from within the app (the Drive panel's "Disconnect" button), or directly from Google's own account settings at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Disconnecting removes JARVIS's local copy of your access token immediately; Google's own page lets you revoke access from Google's side as well.

Other data JARVIS handles

JARVIS is primarily a tool for working with Claude Code and your own local code projects. Depending on which optional features you turn on, it may also read local project files (to show you their contents, never uploading them anywhere without your action), connect to a Telegram bot you configure yourself (for remote notifications/control), or connect to your own ClickUp account (read-only, for task syncing). Each of these is off by default and requires your own explicit setup.

Changes to this policy
